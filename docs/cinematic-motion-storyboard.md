# Moonlit Coast — motion storyboard

All names, dates, and venue references are fictional demo content. The invitation keeps native document scrolling; scenes are not pinned on mobile.

## Prologue — the moonlit title

- **Layers:** coastal-estate photograph, moon disc, wash, film grain, title, invitation copy, framed CTA.
- **Entrance:** photograph eases forward, moon expands into the wash, and title/copy dissolve upward in staggered beats.
- **Living composition:** slow independent title-arrow drift and a shallow photograph parallax.
- **Transition:** wash becomes transparent while the estate scene is made available beneath it.
- **Mobile:** same story with smaller depth travel and no heavy blur/filter animation.

## Chapter 01 — the invitation

- **Layers:** estate image, foreground light bloom, gold contour line, text panel, scroll cue.
- **Entrance:** image scales from a close crop, then overline, title, body and cue resolve in sequence.
- **Living composition:** foreground bloom and contour line float independently; estate travels at a slower rate than the text.
- **Transition:** the contour draws toward the next chapter while the estate settles backward.
- **Mobile:** copy remains in a readable lower-third panel; depth movement is limited to a few pixels.

## Chapter 02 — the two of us

- **Layers:** couple photograph, two orbit rings, floral linework, drifting particles, title copy.
- **Entrance:** picture opens through a mask, rings trace/rotate into alignment, flowers bloom from scale, then typography resolves word by word.
- **Living composition:** photo, particles and rings travel at distinct scroll rates.
- **Transition:** rings widen and the mask settles to lead into the circular countdown composition.
- **Mobile:** mask and bloom remain; lateral movement becomes short vertical travel to protect the crop.

## Chapter 03 — save the night

- **Layers:** dusk feast image, two celestial rings, four number cells, date divider, floating stars.
- **Entrance:** rings draw into place, countdown cells roll up in a stagger, then the date line appears.
- **Living composition:** rings rotate in opposite directions and stars drift subtly while the count remains live.
- **Transition:** ring scale opens outward to reveal the celebration scene.
- **Mobile:** number cells have short staggered motion with no pinning or large scaling.

## Chapter 04 — the celebration

- **Layers:** feast image, candle glints, title column, event rail, schedule rows, gold rule.
- **Entrance:** a dark veil parts horizontally; heading rises from the left while the event rail traces and schedule rows cascade from the right.
- **Living composition:** glints pulse at different timings; image moves slowly opposite the schedule card.
- **Transition:** rail extends into the map route of the venue scene.
- **Mobile:** card enters vertically after the title to preserve tap/reading space.

## Chapter 05 — find your way

- **Layers:** abstract coast-map grid, route, pin halo, venue card, directional rule.
- **Entrance:** coast lines draw, route travels to the pin, halo pulses once, then the card opens from a mask and its typography resolves.
- **Living composition:** coast grid drifts and the pin breathes at a low amplitude.
- **Transition:** route fades into a star trail for RSVP.
- **Mobile:** route/pin remain, with a compact card-mask transition and no expensive map displacement.

## Chapter 06 — kindly reply

- **Layers:** star field, two floral ornaments, RSVP title, form frame, field rules, submit control.
- **Entrance:** stars brighten, ornaments unfurl, copy rises, form frame draws, then fields and button appear in sequence.
- **Living composition:** stars drift and flowers rotate independently at low speed.
- **Transition:** footer settles as the closing frame; native controls remain immediately interactive.
- **Mobile:** elements stack in reading order and use small vertical staging only.

## Accessibility and performance

- `prefers-reduced-motion` reveals the fully readable experience without animated travel.
- Desktop uses wider depth travel; mobile uses shorter movement, shorter stagger, and fewer scrubbed properties.
- Only transforms, opacity, clip paths, and SVG-like stroke/dash effects are animated. No scene is pinned on mobile.
